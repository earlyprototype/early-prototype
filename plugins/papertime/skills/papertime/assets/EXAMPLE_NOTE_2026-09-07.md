# Whether the Thursday status meeting should become a written update

*A reading note written 2026-09-07 for the operator of a six person team, in answer to three questions asked in a one to one on 2026-09-03: what the meeting costs, what a written update would and would not replace, and what to do about the two people who say the meeting is the only time they hear from the rest of the team. There is no earlier document on this, so the note stands alone.*

> **Provenance.** The meeting's length and attendance were read from the shared team calendar for the twelve weeks to 2026-09-04. The team's size and roles were read from the team page on the same day. What people say about the meeting comes from my notes on the four one to ones held in August 2026. Nothing was run or measured for this note: there was no survey and no timing exercise. Each claim is marked as established (read today from a source named here), inferred (reasoned from established facts), recalled (general knowledge, not checked today), or speculation.

---

## 1. The answers in brief

**The meeting costs about 8 hours of the team's time each week, a little over 3 percent of the six people's 240 working hours.** Fifty minutes of scheduled time, plus ten minutes each person spends preparing and twenty minutes most people need to return to interrupted work, comes to 80 minutes each. That total is inferred from calendar times and an ordinary estimate of the cost of an interruption, not measured here. Section 2 has the details.

**A written update replaces the reporting but not the noticing.** Almost everything said in the meeting could be written down instead: the state of each piece of work, and what is blocked. What a document cannot do is let someone hear a hesitation and ask about it. Section 3 has the details.

**The two people who value the meeting are describing a different need, and a written update will not meet it.** Both joined in the last six months, and both named the meeting as where they learn what other people do rather than what they finished. That is an onboarding need wearing a status meeting's clothes. Section 4 has the details.

## 2. What the meeting costs

The direct cost is the smaller half. The calendar shows the meeting booked for 45 minutes and running to a median of 50 across the twelve weeks, with all six people present at 10 of the 12 sittings. That is established from the calendar. The table sets the direct cost beside the cost of the interruption, which appears on no calendar. Each figure is minutes per person per week.

| Cost | Minutes | Where the figure comes from |
|---|---|---|
| The meeting itself | 50 | Median of 12 sittings, against the 45 booked |
| Preparing what to say | 10 | Recalled from what people described in August, not timed |
| Returning to interrupted work | 20 | A common estimate for a mid-morning interruption, not measured here |
| Total | 80 | 8 hours across the six of them |

Two limits on those numbers. The 20 minutes for returning to work is recalled rather than established, and the work behind that figure measures knowledge work in general, not this team. And the meeting sits at 10am on a Thursday, inside the morning block most of the team names as its best working time; moving it to 4pm would cut the interruption cost without changing the meeting at all.

## 3. What a written update replaces

The reporting moves cleanly. Of the 50 minutes, roughly 35 are people saying what they finished and what comes next, which is established from my notes on the four sittings I recorded in August. That content survives being written down and gains from it: it becomes searchable, and the people who miss the meeting stop being told everything twice.

The remaining 15 minutes are the part that does not survive. They are questions asked because of how something was said, and the small negotiations that follow. Whether 15 minutes of that is worth 8 hours is the operator's judgement rather than mine, and the answer probably depends on how much of the team's work crosses between people in a given month. Speculation, offered as a way to test that: in a month where two people share a system the meeting earns its cost, and in a month where six people are on six things it does not.

## 4. The people who would lose something

Two of the six said the meeting is the only time they hear from the rest of the team, and both joined within the last six months. That is established from the August notes.

What they need is a picture of who does what, which the meeting supplies as a side effect of doing something else. A written update supplies the same facts and none of the picture, so removing the meeting without replacing that leaves them worse off than they are now. This is an inference from what they said, not a request either of them made.

## 5. What remains, and what needs the operator's decision

What happened: this note put a number on the meeting's cost, separated what a written update replaces from what it does not, and named who would lose something. What it means: the meeting is a reporting mechanism carrying a second job, and the reporting half moves to a document cheaply while the second half does not. That reading is inferred from twelve weeks of calendar entries and four conversations, and it would not survive the team changing shape.

What remains, in order of information per unit cost, none of it started:

1. Ask the six to write the update for two weeks while keeping the meeting, then see what the meeting still contains. Two weeks, no cost beyond the writing.
2. Time the return to interrupted work for one week rather than using a recalled figure. One week, a few minutes a day per person.
3. Ask the two newer people what they would want instead, rather than inferring it. Two conversations.

What needs the operator's decision:

- Whether to run the two week trial in step 1 before deciding anything. My recommendation, offered: yes, because it produces the only evidence that would change the answer.
- Whether the picture the two newer people are missing is this meeting's job or onboarding's. That is a judgement about what the team is for, and only the operator can make it.
- If the meeting stays, whether to move it out of the morning block. That costs nothing and removes a quarter of the total.
